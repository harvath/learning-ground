UNIT A · THE ECONOMIC STRUCTURE — LESSON 0007

The bubble debate, held open on purpose

This closes Unit A with the meta-skill the other six lessons were building toward: reading a claim, a number, or a term without either swallowing it whole or dismissing it reflexively.

AGI — the clearest worked example

AGI (artificial general intelligence) is the term for an AI system that matches or exceeds human performance across essentially the full range of cognitive tasks — not just narrow ones. Today's models are enormously capable at specific things (writing, coding, some kinds of reasoning), but "AGI" is a claim about generality, not raw capability. There is no agreed technical definition or benchmark for it — labs, researchers, and critics genuinely disagree about what would even count as reaching it, which is a big part of why it functions as much as a strategic and rhetorical term as a technical one.

why this is the perfect worked example AGI isn't just marketing language — it's written directly into contracts with real money attached. Amazon's stake from OpenAI's March 2026 round includes $35B contingent on an IPO/AGI-milestone condition, and Microsoft's technology-access rights to OpenAI's models are bounded through 2032 except for a specific carve-out covering any future model verified to be AGI.[1][2] Labs also use the term differently on purpose: OpenAI's stated mission centers on AGI explicitly, while Anthropic more often says "powerful AI" instead — partly to sidestep exactly this definitional fight. A term with no agreed definition, functioning as a literal legal trigger for tens of billions of dollars, is exactly the kind of claim this lesson is about: real stakes, genuinely unsettled meaning.

The bubble debate — both sides are credible

The Bank for International Settlements' 2026 Annual Economic Report named an AI capex bust, the collapse of circular financing (A6), and record sovereign debt as the top three risks to global financial stability.[3] Gary Marcus (A4) called September 2025 "peak bubble" and predicted 2026 would be the year "it all falls apart." Neither claim is fringe — but neither is settled fact either. Nvidia and Broadcom are posting genuine, current, audited profits, not projections (A1) — that's real too. Both things are simultaneously true: the infrastructure layer is making real money right now, and serious people think the labs layer's valuations don't reflect reality yet. Resist the pull to pick a side for the sake of having an opinion; the honest position in mid-2026 is that this is genuinely unresolved.

Even funded companies fail — the base rate

CB Insights estimates 90% of AI startups that raised money in 2022–2024 will be dead or significantly restructured by the end of 2026. Of more than 14,000 AI startups launched in 2024, 3,800 shut down in 2025 (27%) and another 1,800 in early 2026 (13%) — a 40% failure rate in under two years.[4] Concrete examples: Builder.ai, once valued at $1.2B with Microsoft as a backer, filed for bankruptcy; Yupp.ai, which had raised $33M from well-known investors, shut down in under a year. A funding announcement is evidence a company raised money — it is not evidence the company will still exist next year.

the actual skill, stated plainly Every lesson in this unit has been building toward one habit: when you hit a number, a valuation, a "we've reached X" claim, or a newly popular term, ask (1) who benefits from you believing this, (2) is there an agreed definition/benchmark behind it, and (3) what would the number look like if it were wrong. That's not cynicism — plenty of claims in this unit hold up under that test (Nvidia's profits are real; n8n's SAP deal is real evidence of a moat). The skill is applying the same scrutiny in both directions, not defaulting to belief or defaulting to dismissal.

Check it sticks

① Why does it matter, specifically for reading AI-economy headlines, that "AGI" has no agreed definition?

Because the term gets treated as a fixed technical milestone even though it isn't one — including inside real contracts (Amazon's $35B AGI-contingent tranche, Microsoft's post-2032 AGI carve-out from OpenAI). Without a fixed definition, "reaching AGI" is partly a negotiated, interpreted event rather than a clean fact you could just look up.

② What's the most defensible position on the AI bubble debate as of mid-2026, based on this unit?

The lesson deliberately doesn't resolve this. Serious, credible evidence exists on both sides, applying to different layers (infrastructure profits are real and current; labs-layer valuations are the contested part) — the honest reading holds both without forcing a verdict.
