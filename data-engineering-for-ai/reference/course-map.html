Course map

The full syllabus, by unit. The course follows the spine bottom-up: store → describe → govern → make reachable → act on. One running example org (Meridian) threads through all of it. Slots beyond the current lesson are planned and may shift to fit where you are.

the spine A single piece of Meridian's data — say, a signed customer contract (a PDF) — travels the whole course: it gets stored (A), described with metadata and lineage (B), governed by classification and policy (C), made reachable to a model via embeddings and retrieval (D), and finally acted on by an agent or workflow (E). Each unit owns one segment.
Unit A — Foundations: what data engineering is, and why AI raises the stakes
A1 now The Lifecycle, and Why AI Reframes It The five-stage data engineering lifecycle, its undercurrents, and the "7% AI-ready" gap this course closes. Meet Meridian.
A2 next The Shape of Org Data Structured vs. semi- vs. unstructured; systems of record vs. derived copies. Inventory Meridian's real data.
A3 planned Where Data Lives Database vs. warehouse vs. lake vs. lakehouse; OLTP vs. OLAP. Why the answer isn't "one big folder."
Unit B — Describing data: metadata, catalogs, lineage, contracts
B1planned Metadata — the Four Kinds Technical, business, operational, and active metadata. What each answers, and why agents need all four.
B2planned Catalogs & the Semantic Layer Finding data you don't already know exists; giving metrics one governed definition. The "context layer" claim, weighed.
B3planned Lineage & Provenance Tracing where a value came from. OpenLineage/Marquez (deeper than ai-economy B2's landscape pass).
B4planned Data Contracts & Quality Schemas as promises; expectations/tests before anything downstream trusts the data.
Unit C — Governing data: policy, classification, access
C1planned Classification & Sensitivity PII/PHI, tiers, tagging. You can't govern what you haven't labelled.
C2planned Policy-as-Code RBAC vs. ABAC; OPA/Rego vs. Cedar. Rules that live in version control, not a wiki. (Cross-ref ai-economy B5.)
C3planned Governance for Agents "Can find it" vs. "is allowed to act on it." Decision governance — the underrated 2026 gap.
Unit D — Making data reachable by a model
D1planned Embeddings & Vector Search Turning meaning into geometry; nearest-neighbour retrieval. Built from foundations.
D2planned Chunking & Indexing How Meridian's contracts and docs get cut up so retrieval returns the right slice.
D3planned RAG vs. Long-Context vs. Fine-Tuning Three ways to get data into a model; GraphRAG. When to use which. (Cross-ref ai-economy B3.)
D4planned MCP & Tools How an agent actually reaches governed data at runtime — and where policy gets enforced.
Unit E — Agents & workflows over governed data
E1planned Agent vs. Workflow Open-ended reasoning vs. deterministic pipeline. When each is the right tool.
E2planned The Framework Landscape Mastra, LlamaIndex, LangGraph and peers — placed in the stack, none crowned. (Cross-ref ai-economy B4.)
E3planned Putting It Together One governed retrieval workflow over Meridian, end to end. The spine's payoff.
