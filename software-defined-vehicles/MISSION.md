# Mission: Software-Defined Vehicles — History, Players, and the Current State

## Why
The user needs working fluency in the software-defined vehicle (SDV) industry to competently engage with a client operating in this space. The specific client and engagement are confidential, but the underlying goal is clear: understand the industry's history, name the real players correctly, and read its current state — who's ahead, what's hype, what's settled — well enough to hold a substantive conversation and make informed judgments. This sits alongside a general desire for industry literacy and a business/investment lens, not a need for deep automotive engineering skill.

## Success looks like
- Can define "software-defined vehicle" precisely enough to notice when someone else's definition is fuzzier or narrower than their own, and knows the concept has no clean single origin story
- Can explain why the industry moved from distributed ECU architectures to centralized/zonal ones, and why that shift is what actually enables OTA updates and subscription features
- Can place any OEM, Tier-1 supplier, or platform vendor named in a client conversation into the right layer of the industry (OEM / Tier-1 supplier / chip-compute / software platform / cloud) and describe its current SDV position
- Can discuss the VW/Cariad story and the Rivian–VW deal in enough detail to use them as a reference case for "what happens when in-house software development doesn't scale"
- Can speak to why China's automakers are widely regarded as ahead on SDV right now, citing real evidence, while knowing where that consensus is thin
- Knows the regulatory skeleton (UNECE WP.29 R155/R156, EU Data Act) well enough to recognize when it's relevant to a client's situation
- Knows which 2–3 sources to keep checking after the course ends, since this domain moves fast and will decay

## Constraints
- The client relationship and its specifics are confidential — teaching should stay at general-industry level, never attempt to probe or infer client details
- **Landscape-first.** Technical architecture concepts (E/E architecture, AUTOSAR, zonal computing) are explained only as much as needed to understand the business moves and players — this is not a deep engineering course
- Every claim must be dated and cited to a real, checkable source — this is a fast-moving industry and the material needs to be trustworthy enough to repeat in a professional context
- Keep lessons short and tightly scoped — one lesson, one win

## Out of scope
- Deep automotive engineering (writing AUTOSAR code, ECU programming, embedded systems development)
- Autonomous driving / ML technical internals, beyond what's needed to understand AD as a business/product feature
- Investment advice or stock-picking on specific companies
